# Homepage Copywriting Framework (full method)

Source: Viktor's copywriting guide. This is the canonical method the
`website-copy-reframe` skill operationalizes. Canvas template:
the copywriting canvas Sheet (kept in the private workspace).

## Why homepages fail

A homepage is a **marketing asset**, but it gets pulled 4 ways:
- Founders want it to show ALL services + ALL industries, as broad as possible.
- Delivery wants it to showcase technologies.
- Sales wants it to showcase collaboration benefits.
- HR wants it to showcase amazing corporate culture.

Result: vague, generic, ambiguous messaging. For a service business of any size, the
homepage's real job is to answer 5 fundamentals for the target market:
1. Which services does the company provide?
2. How does it work?
3. What exactly gets done?
4. When would I use it?
5. How does it help me make progress on what I'm trying to do?

Until most of the target market can answer these easily, resist vague messaging.

## Step 1 — Site structure of a service company (minimum)

1. **Homepage** — Value Prop · Capabilities · Problems · Customer logos
2. **Case studies** — Industry · Geo · Service · Stack
3. **Company page** — Your Team · How we work · Why choose you · Awards & Certification
4. **Service pages** — one per service/industry/geo · summary + benefits + problems it
   fixes + social proof + CTA · great for SEO
5. **Pricing** — back pricing with social proof · FAQ with common objections · cost of inaction
6. **Resources** — Problem-unaware: webinars, ultimate guides, symptom lists ·
   Problem-aware: templates, quizzes, scorecards · Solution-aware: solution comparisons,
   ROI calculators

## Step 2 — Choose your audience

Map candidate segments on 3 axes: **company type × person/department × situation**.

- Tying messaging to a persona (e.g. "agency owner") or company type (e.g. "service IT
  companies") = **vertical** positioning.
- Targeting a shared **situation** across many company types = **horizontal** positioning.
- For custom dev / any high-ticket service with a complex sales cycle, horizontal is
  **not relevant if marketing budget < $50K** → focus on **vertical**.
- A "situation" can be a process ("I'm patching a hole in drywall") or a desired outcome
  ("I'm trying to get more leads"). Either way the target must be **actively spending
  energy** on that goal.

Prioritize among audience options with:
- Where do we want growth in the next 12 months?
- Which group can we realistically reach with current go-to-market?
- Are we creating or capturing demand?

## Step 3 — Bind the champion's situation to activities

Common mistake: writing for the buyer / decision-maker (an exec high in the org). They
have authority but are usually too far from the problem to care about your product.

B2B cycles run 3–6 months → write for the **champion**: someone with time, energy, and
motivation to convince stakeholders, work with legal/procurement, and push the deal
through. The champion isn't always the end user but must be close enough to the problem
to see its negative consequences and how the product solves them.

Emphasize what the champion is **trying to do** (their "situation"). Link the product's
capabilities/features/benefits to that current situation.

First answers are too high-level. "Increase revenue" / "save time" isn't enough. List
**functional activities**, not outcomes:
- "Ensure compliance" = outcome → activity = run reviews with staff, standardize procedures.
- "Reduce downtime" = outcome → activities = routine bug-fixing, load-balancing,
  health & performance monitoring.

Activities must be **tool-agnostic**: "wash hands" can be done with sanitizer, soap,
water, etc. Once you have the big picture of what the champion is trying to do, **cross
out everything unrelated to your services**. On remaining levels keep detailing until a
**slightly uncomfortable level of specificity** — that's where it resonates.

## Step 4 — Constraints & problems of the current path (left half of canvas)

Having chosen audience + champion + the right level of specificity, document their
**current way** of handling the situation, plus the **limitations** and **problems** of
that method. Read the canvas left→right, then vertically down columns (like a user-story
map). Best used for ONE primary use case.

The current way should have limitations your service improves. Limitations are neither
inherently positive nor negative — but show how they create **problems** for the champion.

## Step 5 — Map capabilities, features, benefits (right half)

Three concepts (a reference example):
- **Capabilities** — what someone actually does with your service.
  *Example:* "Generate pipeline and close more deals".
- **Features** — technical aspects that enable the capabilities.
  *Example:* "With detailed SOPs, frameworks, templates, and experience to help you reach targets".
- **Benefits** — the result of applying the capabilities.
  *Example:* "to 2x New Revenue with relevant leads that fit your ICP".

Load-bearing links:
- Your **capabilities improve the limitations** of the prospect's current approach.
- Your **benefits solve (or remove) the problem** blocking progress in the prospect's situation.

## Step 6 — Translate strategy into copy

Best blocks for a service business:
- **Hero** — in the canvas, this is the top-row message + key capabilities. Include the
  service category + a trust-building section.
- **Problem Section** — makes the visitor feel understood ("I'm in the right place"),
  repels bad-fit, attracts good-fit. Pulled from current-way / limitations / problems cells.
- **Solution** — 3 arguments to convince the visitor to take the next step (submit a form
  or book a call). Drawn from capabilities/features/benefits (canvas rows 2–4).

Lower half adapts to the company and routes visitors to other pages:
- Services block → service pages
- Industries
- Testimonials
- Why work with us
- Persona-based segmentation ("here's how sales teams use it, here's how marketing teams…")
- Company-based segmentation ("here's how manufacturing uses it, here's how healthcare…")
- ROI section (numeric results)
- Calendar / Form
